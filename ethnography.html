ETHNOGRAPHY

Over the course of four days, I observed the patrons of Galaxy Theatres Gig Harbor. There, I learned all about the WIDE variety of people who go watch movies in theaters. The theater is situated in Uptown, a pretty urban area. “Urban” is a bit of a stretch though, considering we live in a relatively small town.

*DAY 1* Walking up to the building, I noticed a couple things that made this place stand out. A bright glowing Luxury+ sign, along with elaborate stonework suggests this isn’t your average Cinemark. I walked into the lobby at about 4PM. Huge. Bright. Colorful. There’s a lot of blinking lights, neon, LEDs, everything. I notice a couple things right away: 1. There’s a bunch of people here, but it’s not overly crowded. Everyone must be in their movies by now 2. Decorations seem to be a cool mix of retro and modern. The galaxy-themed carpets and old-school marquee would suggest this building was built in the late 2000’s, but the color changing LED panels, TVs displaying movie trailers, and ads for a mobile app say this business has adapted to the current digital era. 3. People seem to be dressed casually. No formal attire, cosplay, or church attire in sight. I’ll do some more observations the next day, focusing on the people this time.

*DAY 2* This is the only theater in town, so it tends to gain a lot of foot traffic. This includes people of all ages, genders, ethnicities, really anyone’s welcome here, provided you don’t act like a tool. More on that later. The most common people I see here are elder folks and families, especially families with younger children. Almost everyone here is in some kind of a group though. I witnessed one such patron ordering, a middle-aged man and who I presume to be his brother, or maybe a close friend. I mentioned earlier about people acting rude sometimes, and this is a major taboo. Unfortunately, people don’t really face any consequences for disrespecting retail and service staff, and they’re often used as an outlet for people’s frustration and anger.

*DAY 3* Okay, today things get a bit more interesting. I saw a handful of cosplayers for Avatar 3. Cosplay is when somebody dresses up as a character, and it can vary wildly. It can range on a scale from wearing a character’s hat, to 3D printing armor, making your own clothing, or building cardboard replicas of weapons. I was cosplaying as an exhausted teenager trying to finish an English project. We get a consistent rotation of merch, posters, and cardboard standees for the most dedicated of moviegoers. All of these things are used to advertise the movie, but the merch is more important than that. It allows fans to have a keepsake of an important memory of theirs, such as, “the time I saw this movie with my girlfriend”, or, “the time I took my family to see this movie”. It’s really interesting seeing people bond over a shared common interest, and I’m glad my coworkers and I can help make people’s day a bit better.

*DAY 4* People’s sense of scheduling also has a lot of variation. Most people show up right on time, about a half hour before the movie starts. Others show up 2 hours early, and others show up 15 minutes late. Everybody seems to follow the same routine: They walk in, scan their tickets, buy some snacks, and walk into their theater. Another thing to note is how people talk about their favorite movies. A lot of the debates are the usual, Marvel VS D.C, Shin Godzilla VS Godzilla Minus One, Alien VS Predator. But at the end of the day, everyone’s here to watch a movie and have a good time with their friends and family. All in all, I’d say this ethnography was really cool. I thought it was really cool to see all these different people talk about the things they enjoy, and spending time with those close to them to share an important memory.
